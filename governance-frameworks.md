# Governance frameworks

[← Back](README.md)

The documents an AI system gets measured against, grouped by who wrote them, and the
surveys that compare them. Read the surveys first; they will tell you which frameworks to
read in full.

## Surveys

1. [The global landscape of AI ethics guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-019-0088-2) (Jobin, Ienca and Vayena, 2019)
   - *Why*: Eighty-four guideline documents, eleven principles, broad agreement on the
     words and almost none on what they require. The reason a principle without a control
     behind it is not a governance measure.

2. [Responsible AI Pattern Catalogue: A Collection of Best Practices for AI Governance and Engineering](https://doi.org/10.1145/3626234) (Lu, Zhu, Xu, Whittle, Zowghi and Jacquet, 2024)
   - *Why*: The step from principle to pattern: governance, process and product patterns
     you can point to in a design. The bridge between the frameworks below and a system
     diagram.

## Public bodies

3. [Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1) (NIST, 2023)
   - *Why*: Govern, map, measure, manage. The US reference point, voluntary, and the
     structure most other frameworks now map themselves onto.

4. [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Artificial Intelligence Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) (European Parliament and Council, 2024)
   - *Why*: The first binding horizontal AI law. Risk tiers, prohibited practices, and
     obligations on providers and deployers. The text, not a summary of it.

5. [Recommendation of the Council on Artificial Intelligence](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0449) (OECD, 2019, amended 2024)
   - *Why*: The principles most national strategies cite. Short enough to read in full, and
     useful for seeing what forty-odd governments could agree to.

6. [Multilayer Framework for Good Cybersecurity Practices for AI](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/multilayer-framework-for-good-cybersecurity-practices-for-ai) (ENISA, 2023)
   - *Why*: The European security agency's layering of general cybersecurity, AI-specific
     controls and sector rules. The clearest statement that AI security sits on top of
     ordinary security, not beside it.

## Standards

7. [ISO/IEC 42001:2023, Artificial intelligence management system](https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html) (ISO/IEC, 2023)
   - *Why*: The certifiable one. An AI management system in the shape of ISO 27001, which
     is what an auditor will eventually ask for.

8. [ISO/IEC 23053:2022, Framework for AI systems using machine learning](https://www.iso.org/standard/74438.html) (ISO/IEC, 2022)
   - *Why*: The vocabulary and the component model: data, pipeline, model, deployment. The
     shared nouns before a controls conversation.

## Industry

9. [AI Controls Matrix](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/artifacts/ai-controls-matrix) (Cloud Security Alliance, 2025)
   - *Why*: Controls, not principles, mapped to the frameworks above. The nearest thing to
     a checklist for an AI system in production.

10. [Secure AI Framework (SAIF)](https://saif.google/) (Google, 2023 onward)
    - *Why*: A large operator's own security framework for AI, with a risk self-assessment.
      Useful as a view of what a mature security organisation thinks the controls are.

11. [Databricks AI Security Framework (DASF)](https://www.databricks.com/resources/whitepaper/databricks-ai-security-framework-dasf) (Databricks, 2024, updated 2025)
    - *Why*: Risks enumerated per component of an ML system, each with controls. The most
      concrete of the vendor frameworks, and readable without using the product.
